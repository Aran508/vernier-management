The one rule that decides whether this gets finished

Every phase must end in something that works on its own. A room that only lights up after all six phases are done is a room that never lights up. Phase 0 is a talking assistant with memory and no hardware at all — build that first, live with it for a week, and let what annoys you decide Phase 1.

01

System architecture

5 layers

Two machines, split by duty cycle. The Pi is always on and draws about 5 W — it owns the ears and the room. The laptop is the brain and sleeps when you do. That split is what makes "always listening" survive your electricity bill.

Teal is software and data. Vermilion is anything physical that can be unplugged. Every arrow is one protocol — if you cannot name the protocol on a link, that link is not designed yet.

02

What "alive" actually means

6 ingredients

You asked for it to be sentient. It won't be — but that isn't what you're actually after. What makes an assistant feel present rather than transactional is six specific, buildable behaviours. Skip any one of them and it drops straight back to feeling like a chatbot in a nice frame.

IngredientWhy it landsBuilt with
Persistent memoryIt says "you said the fixture tolerance was the problem yesterday — did that hold?" Nothing else buys this much perceived intelligence for so little code.SQLite + sqlite-vec
Proactive initiativeIt speaks first. This is the single biggest one. An assistant that waits for input is a tool; one that opens with "your 3P review is in 40 minutes and the BOM still has two blanks" is a colleague.scheduler + triggers
ProsodyA flat robotic voice destroys the illusion instantly, no matter how good the reasoning is. It needs pacing, emphasis, and a shift in register.Piper / ElevenLabs
Presence awarenessKnows you walked in, knows you left. Dims the room, pauses the brief mid-sentence, resumes when you sit back down.LD2410 mmWave
Sentiment readingYour voice tone sets its register — terse and factual when you're stressed, expansive when you're exploring. This is the "sentiment" half of what you asked for, and it's real.openSMILE / wav2vec
State continuityThe dashboards never reset to a blank welcome screen. It is always mid-conversation with your life.event-sourced core
Design note

Memory needs three separate stores, not one. Episodic — what happened and when, appended forever. Semantic — durable facts about you, your projects, the people you work with, rewritten as they change. Procedural — how you do things: your 3P sheet format, your review checklist, your naming convention. The third one is what turns a generic assistant into yours, and almost nobody builds it.

03

The wake sequence

3 claps → live room

This is the moment the whole project is judged on. Get the timing wrong and there's an eight-second silence after your claps where nothing happens and the magic dies. The fix is on line four of the table.

T+ActorEvent
0.00 sPiThird clap detected. Three broadband transients, 150–600 ms apart, ≥12 dB over the rolling noise floor, each decaying inside 100 ms.
0.05 sPiCross-check mmWave: is anyone actually in the room? If not, ignore — this kills the door-slam and dropped-spanner false positives.
0.15 sPiLED tubes ease 0 → 35%, warm white, 800 ms curve. The room responds before anything else does.
0.30 sPiPi speaks the greeting itself, from pre-rendered local audio. It does not wait for the laptop. This one decision is the difference between "alive" and "buffering".
0.35 sPiWake-on-LAN magic packet to the laptop; DDC-CI power-on to each monitor over the display cable.
3–8 sCoreLaptop up. Kiosk browsers restore their assigned dashboard per monitor. Panels populate as data arrives — never a spinner on a blank screen.
4 sCoreBrief assembled in parallel: calendar, unread mail triage, open tasks, overnight production numbers, two news items filtered to your industry.
6 sCoreSpoken brief begins, LEDs to full. Ends on a question — "what's the one thing that has to land today?" — and your answer sets the day's priority.
+10 sPiClap trigger debounced for 10 minutes. Wake word stays live.
Failure mode to design around

Clap detection will false-positive on a dropped tool or a slammed door. Three guards, in order of cheapness: require the three-transient rhythm (not just loudness), gate on presence, and make the wake action harmless — lights and a greeting, never anything destructive. Never put an irreversible action behind an acoustic trigger.

Buying used without regret

Three rules. Never used: power supplies, storage, anything mains-connected — these fail silently and take other things with them. Used freely: monitors, arms, speakers, chassis — defects are visible on inspection. Test before paying: a laptop's battery cycle count and thermal throttling under a 10-minute load are the two things sellers don't mention and you can't fix.

Why C is worth the ₹9,000

Pepper's Ghost has one hard constraint that shapes the UI: black is transparent, white is solid. Anything dark in your design simply isn't there. So the holo panel gets its own layout — the avatar, a clock, three status lines, thin bright strokes on pure black — while the dense dashboards stay on the flat monitors where they're actually readable. Don't try to mirror a dashboard into the glass; it will look like grey soup.

06

Software stack

Repo layout

One Python core on the laptop, one Python agent on the Pi, one React dashboard served to every screen. Everything talks over an internal event bus, which means you can add a surface later without touching the core.

Core — laptop

Python 3.12, FastAPI, asyncio event bus. Anthropic SDK for reasoning, Ollama for the always-on cheap path, faster-whisper for STT, Piper for local TTS. SQLite with sqlite-vec for all three memory stores.

Edge — Pi 5

Python, openWakeWord, a 40-line clap detector on raw audio frames, LD2410 over UART, MQTT to the core, WLED over HTTP, wakeonlan and ddcutil for the room.

Dashboard

React + Vite, one route per monitor, WebSocket subscription with JSON-patch deltas so panels update without re-rendering. Chromium in kiosk mode, one instance per display.

Room hub

Home Assistant in Docker, if you add more than about four devices. It already speaks Zigbee, Tuya, Matter and MQTT — do not hand-roll device drivers you can inherit.

07

Self-connecting tools

MCP + the gate

You want it to find software it needs, try it, and connect itself. That is exactly what MCP is for, and it's how I'm working with you right now. But there is one step you can't remove.

StepActionDetail
1Recognise the gapA task fails or is refused for want of a capability. The failure is logged as a structured need, not just an error.
2SearchQuery MCP registries and package indexes for a server matching the need.
3Read the manifestPull the declared tool list, the permission surface, and what it wants to reach on the network.
4Install isolatedInto a throwaway container. No host filesystem, no credentials, no LAN.
5ProbeEnumerate tools, call the read-only ones with synthetic input, record what actually works versus what the README claims.
6Approval gateA card on the dashboard: what it wants, why, what it will be able to reach. Read-only local tools auto-approve. Anything touching network, credentials or your files waits for you — once.
7RegisterPersist to the tool router with a scoped credential and a per-tool rate limit. Available from the next turn onward.
Why step 6 stays

An MCP server is arbitrary code that runs with whatever access you hand it. Auto-installing whatever a search returns means one poisoned README or one prompt injection inside a document you asked it to read is enough to get your CATIA models, your mail and your credentials exfiltrated — and you would not see it happen. The gate costs you one click per new tool, roughly once a week. It is the cheapest insurance in this entire build.

The right framing: discovery is autonomous, installation is consented. That is still a system that grows its own capabilities — it just tells you when it does.

Domain automation

CATIA · CAE · 3P

Everything above is infrastructure. This sheet is the part that pays for itself, because it automates the work you actually do. Build it last, but design the core knowing it's coming.

One practical caution

CATIA models, 3P sheets and plant figures are usually your employer's property, not yours. Before any of that goes to a cloud API, check what your workplace actually permits — and consider running the domain layer against a local model on that data, keeping Claude for your personal side. Worth settling in week one rather than week twelve.

Running cost

Per month

The hardware is one payment. This is the recurring one, and it is almost entirely under your control through model routing and caching.

PathModelUsed for$ / MTok in·out
Hard reasoningclaude-opus-5Design questions, CAE interpretation, multi-step tool work, anything you'd want a colleague for. 1M context, adaptive thinking on.5 · 25
Routineclaude-haiku-4-5Intent routing, mail triage, summarising, classification. Most of your call volume, a fifth of the price.1 · 5
Always-on / offlineollama · localWake handling, quick lookups, and everything that must keep working when the internet doesn't. Free, runs on the laptop GPU.0

The three levers that actually move the bill

  • Prompt caching. Your system prompt plus the semantic memory prefix is large and barely changes between calls — cache it and you stop paying full price for it on every turn. This is the single biggest saving and it costs you nothing in quality. Keep volatile content (timestamps, the current question) after the last cache breakpoint or you silently invalidate the whole thing.
  • Effort control. Set output_config.effort per route — low for the morning brief, high or xhigh for design and simulation reasoning. Same model, very different spend.
  • Route before you reason. Let the local model decide whether a request even needs the cloud. Most of them don't.

Realistic personal use with caching and routing in place lands around $20–60/month (₹1,700–5,000). Without them, easily five times that. Instrument usage on every response from day one — if cache_read_input_tokens comes back zero, something in your prefix is changing and you are paying for it.

Before I start building

Phase 0 needs no hardware and no purchases, so I can start on it immediately. Two decisions change its shape:

  1. Cloud, local, or hybrid? Hybrid is my recommendation — Claude for reasoning, a local model for the always-on path and anything work-confidential. It's more moving parts, but it's the only option that survives both a power cut and a company data policy.
  2. Windows or Linux on the brain laptop? This one is load-bearing: pycatia needs Windows COM to talk to CATIA. If the domain layer matters to you, the laptop runs Windows and the Pi carries the always-on Linux work.
